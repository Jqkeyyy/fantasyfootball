What the numbers actually say

Rebuilt on your Sleeper ADP file and the real 2026 bye schedule, then run 40,000 times. Four things hold up.

1
Keep pick 2. Do not trade it for 15.
Pick 2 survives 9.82 weeks and wins the league 6.39% of the time. Pick 15 survives 9.08 and wins 4.62%. The gap is +0.74 weeks ±0.037 when both slots are measured inside the same simulated league — about twenty standard errors, and it reproduced on three independent seeds. Slot value falls almost perfectly from 1 to 18 (correlation −0.97).
2
Bye weeks are the risk you actually control on draft night.
Four of your players on bye in the same week doubles your chance of being chopped that week (18.7% vs 9.2%). Five or more triples it (29.6%). Week 11 is the trap: 41 of the 234 drafted players are off that week, and a team with five out in week 11 gets chopped 31.5% of the time. There is no week 12 bye in 2026.
3
Never leave the draft with one QB and one TE.
Going into the season one-deep at both cost 0.44 weeks at pick 2 and 0.58 at pick 15. Carrying both backups was the only roster rule that improved on default drafting. Twenty-eight of the roughly 32 startable quarterbacks get drafted here, so the best free-agent QB is worth about 7.9 points a week against the 13.6 your worst real starter gives you.
4
The waiver wire is still where the biggest edge lives.
Hoarding FAAB returned 3 points of season value. Bidding hard on the few genuine upgrades returned 82. That dwarfs anything available to you on draft night — and unlike your draft, almost nobody plays it well.
This corrects what I told you first
My first pass said pick 2 and pick 15 were a coin flip. That was wrong, and not just from noise. In that version the other seventeen teams were drawn from a bank of pre-drafted rosters instead of drafting live, so your team never actually competed with them for the same players — which is exactly the mechanism that makes an early slot valuable. Real Sleeper ADP and a genuine shared pool make the gap appear immediately.
The plan in one line
Keep pick 2, take the best player on the board every round, spread your byes, refuse to finish with one QB or one TE, and save your real aggression for the wire.

Why an early slot wins a snake draft

The snake is supposed to make this fair, and on pick numbers it does: your thirteen picks average 129.3 versus 130.1 for slot 15. Nearly identical. But value is not linear in draft position.

Identical pick sum, 57 points apart. Player #2 is worth far more than player #15, while player #35 is only slightly worse than player #22. A dispersed pair of picks beats a bunched pair with the same average whenever the value curve is convex — and in an 18-team league it is steeply convex, because replacement level sits so low.

Bye Weeks

Eight starters, five bench spots, and a free-agent pool with nothing in it. When three or four of your players are off in the same week, you are not optimising a lineup — you are trying to field one.

Chance of being chopped, by players on bye that week

From 6,000 simulated leagues. Baseline is 9.2% — roughly one in eleven, averaged over a season as the field shrinks. One player out is free; the damage starts at three and turns severe at five.

What to do about it at the draft
Keep a running count from around round six. Cap yourself at three players on any one week and treat a fourth as a reason to take the next name instead — the ADP difference between two players a few picks apart is far smaller than the survival cost of stacking them. Watch weeks 11, 8 and 10 hardest, and note that your two quarterbacks and two tight ends should never share a bye, since those are the slots you cannot cover from the wire.
